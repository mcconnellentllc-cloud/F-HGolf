2026 Couples Scramble

Rules & Format

F&H Golf Course · Aug 22

← Back to tournaments

The Couples Scramble is a one-day, two-couple team scramble — four players per team. All four tee off, the team picks the best ball, and all four play their next shots from that spot — continuing until the ball is holed. One score per hole, recorded on the team’s scorecard. No Calcutta.

1. Format & scramble mechanics

  • Two-couple team scramble — four players per team. All four tee off; the team selects the best of the four drives. Non-selected balls are picked up.
  • All four players play from the same location — every shot. After the drive is selected, all four hit their next shot from the spot of the selected ball. Continue like that from tee to green: play the best of the four, all four hit again from that spot, repeat until the ball is holed. One team score per hole.
  • On the putting green — marker convention:
    • Mark the selected ball by placing a marker one putter-head length away from the ball’s spot (keeps the marker out of teammates’ putting lines).
    • All four players putt from the same placement. Set the ball back down at the same reference point off the marker for each teammate. Whichever line, break, or hand you prefer — put every ball in the same spot so every putt is the same putt.
    • Once any team member holes the selected ball, the hole is complete.
  • Play the full 18 holes. One-day tournament — gross decides flight winners.
  • Shotgun start: 8:00 AM. Report to your assigned hole 10 minutes early.
  • Flighting: a coin flip mid-round decides front-9 (Out) or back-9 (In). That 9-hole gross places your team in a flight; scorecard playoff on the same 9 breaks any tie for 1st. Everyone still plays the full 18.
  • No Calcutta. Flight prizes only.

2. Out of Bounds OB

Hole #1 — both sides. Out of bounds runs down both the east and west sides of the fairway on hole one.

  • West side is marked by white stakes. The OB line runs only as far north as the northernmost white stake. If your ball comes to rest north of that last stake and still to the west, you’re in play.
  • The exterior fence of the entire golf course is out of bounds. Any ball beyond the fence line, on any hole, is OB.

Penalty: stroke and distance — add one penalty stroke and replay from where the previous shot was played (USGA Rule 18.2). If you’re unsure whether a ball is in or out, play a provisional before leaving the tee/spot; sort it out when you reach the ball.

What “stroke and distance” actually means

It’s a two-part penalty. You’re charged both a stroke (one added to your score) and the distance (you replay from the spot of your previous shot — you don’t get to drop where the ball came to rest).

Example — hole 1 tee shot goes OB west of the white stakes:

StrokeWhat happens
1Drive off the tee — goes OB.
2Penalty stroke.
3Re-tee and hit again.

Your next full shot is your 4th. The OB ball is dead — you don’t play it, and you don’t get to drop near the fence.

Two-man scramble version:

  • If one of the two drives is in bounds, the team simply picks that drive and plays on — no penalty, the OB ball just doesn’t get selected.
  • If both drives go OB, add one penalty stroke to the team score and both players re-tee.
  • If a shot from the selected spot (approach, iron, wedge) goes OB, the team takes S&D: one penalty stroke, both players replay from that selected spot.

Save the walk-back — play a provisional. Any time OB or a lost ball is even a possibility, announce “playing a provisional” before you leave the tee and hit another ball (Rule 18.3). If the first ball is found in bounds, the provisional is picked up — no penalty. If the first is OB or lost, the provisional is already in play and the group didn’t have to wait.

3. Penalty areas & hazards Local

The following on-course areas are played as penalty areas:

  • Between holes #3 and #4 — the low area / hazard running between those fairways.
  • Between holes #6 and #7 — the low area / hazard between those fairways.
  • West of the driving range — the hole / low area on the west side of the range.

Local rule — no grounding of clubs in these penalty areas. Take your address without letting the clubhead touch the ground or any loose impediment. This local rule restores the pre-2019 restriction and is enforced as if the areas were bunkers under USGA Rule 12.2b(1).

Options when your ball is in one of these penalty areas (all cost one penalty stroke):

  • Play it as it lies from inside the penalty area — no penalty, but no grounding the club before the stroke. Grounding = two-stroke penalty.
  • Stroke and distance — replay from where the previous shot was played.
  • Back-on-line relief — keep the point where the ball last crossed the edge of the penalty area between you and the hole, and drop anywhere on that line.
  • Lateral relief (2 club lengths) — drop within two club lengths of the point where the ball last crossed the edge, no closer to the hole.

Relief options track USGA Rule 17. Modern USGA rules allow grounding a club in a penalty area — the Founders committee tightens that back to no-grounding as a local rule.

4. Playing the ball

  • Play the ball down. Play it as it lies — no fluffing (lifting grass under the ball to improve the lie) and no bumping (nudging the ball to a better spot). Improving your lie or line of play is a two-stroke penalty (USGA Rule 8.1).
  • Drops. When taking a drop, drop the ball from higher than knee height. It must come to rest within one club length of the chosen point, and in the same cut of grass as the chosen point (fairway drop lands in fairway, rough drop lands in rough).
  • Tree relief (young trees). If your ball comes to rest close to a tree whose trunk is less than 4″ in diameter, take two club lengths of relief, no closer to the hole. Protecting the young trees is critical — always take the relief rather than risk damaging a trunk or roots.
  • Unplayable ball (anywhere except a penalty area): one penalty stroke, then choose stroke-and-distance, back-on-line, or two-club-length lateral relief (USGA Rule 19).

5. Pace of play, etiquette & ties

Pace of play matters. This is a shotgun-start, one-day tournament — every group finishes when the slowest group finishes, and the meal + awards wait on the field. Target pace is ~4:15 for 18. Your group’s reference point is the group ahead of you — keep pace with them, not with the group behind.

Play a provisional every time OB is even a possibility. A provisional ball costs you nothing if the first ball is found in bounds — and it saves the whole group a 300-yard walk back to the tee if it isn’t. Announce “playing a provisional” before you leave the tee or the previous spot, then hit again. This is the single biggest thing you can do to keep the field moving (Rule 18.3).

Habits that keep the pace up:

  • Ready golf. Whoever is ready hits — honors on the tee is nice, but not worth waiting for.
  • Be ready when it’s your turn. Read your putt while others are reading theirs; pull the club you need while walking to your ball.
  • 3-minute search max for a lost ball (Rule 18.2). After three minutes the ball is lost — go to your provisional (or take stroke and distance).
  • Stay caught up with the group ahead — do not wave anyone through. This is a shotgun start; every group is on the course at the same time and there’s nowhere for the group behind to go. If a gap opens to the group ahead, that’s the signal to step it up — go straight to ready golf and hustle between shots until the gap closes.
  • Don’t scout the whole shot. One look, pick a target, hit it. Scrambles are usually the fastest format because the team can talk strategy while walking — use that.
  • Repair ball marks, replace divots, rake bunkers.
  • No cell-phone calls or music that carries across the course.

Ties — flight prizes only (no Calcutta this tournament)

  • 2nd & 3rd place — split the pot. 2nd and 3rd place gross ties combine the two prize amounts and split evenly among the tied teams. No playoff.
  • 1st place — scorecard playoff. Compare the tied teams’ scores hole-by-hole in Men’s Hdcp difficulty order, using the full 18-hole scorecard. Start with the hardest hole and work down the ranking. The first team with a lower score on a hole wins — take the prize and stop.
    For this course: hole 11 is the hardest, hole 2 is second, hole 15 is third, and so on down the printed handicap column on the scorecard.
    If the walk reaches the end of the card with no decision, the committee calls a chip-off for prize order.

Scorecards must be turned in signed by the scorer and attested by another team member. Cards can’t be changed after they’re turned in.

7. Committee & disputes

If you have a rules question you can’t resolve on the course:

  • Call the pro shop or wave down a committee member. Don’t adjudicate it yourselves and slow the group.
  • If a ruling can’t reach you in a reasonable time, the team may play a second ball under both interpretations (USGA Rule 20.1c(3)). Note which ball was played under which rule on the scorecard and the committee will decide which score counts.
  • Anything not covered above — play by the USGA Rules of Golf. The tournament committee is the final authority on all rulings, ties, and disputes.
